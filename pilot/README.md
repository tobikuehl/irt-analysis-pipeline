# Abstract
**Title:** Modulation of neurogenic inflammation following a controlled thermal stimulus—a randomized, sham-controlled, single-blind pilot study of a novel osteopathic approach

**Background:** Neurogenic inflammation (NI) is mediated by the axon reflex and the dorsal root reflex (DRR). Gate control theory suggests that activation of Aβ fibers can inhibit NI. Flare inhibition (FI) is a manual joint mobilization technique that employs this mechanism; direct evidence that it can reduce the neurogenic inflammatory response is lacking.

**Methods:** In a randomized, controlled, single-blind pilot study, 30 healthy adults were recruited. After exclusion of 10 participants and targeted replacement of 3 participants, 23 were included in the analysis (verum n = 12, sham n = 11). Following a standardized thermal stimulus (45 s, 52 °C) on the dorsum of the hand, the verum group received rhythmic oscillatory mobilization of the joints innervated by the C5–Th1 segments, while the sham group received gentle skin contact. The primary objective was to assess feasibility. As a secondary outcome, skin temperature was recorded over 16 minutes and analyzed.

**Results:** Recruitment rate (15/month), technical failure rate (0.09%), and measurement precision (RMSE 0.24 °C) met the predefined criteria; the dropout rate of 33% exceeded the target value of 15%. Blinding was successful (52.2% correct assignment, p = 0.83). The LMM showed significantly faster cooling in the verum group (p=0.0087). AUC was significantly
lower (p =0.010, Cohen’s d = 1.02, BF₁ ₀ = 6.05).

**Conclusion:** This pilot study supports the feasibility of the study protocol and provides preliminary evidence for a possible modulation of NI by FI. However, the effect size is subject to uncertainty (95% CI: 0.29–∞) and requires confirmation in a confirmatory RCT.

**Registration:** [DRKS00040377](https://www.drks.de/search/de/trial/DRKS00040377/details)
